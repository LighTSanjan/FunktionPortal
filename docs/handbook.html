01What the portal does

The treasurer's month used to be mostly transcription: the same few facts (which classes ran, who came, what each coach is owed, who paid for the term) typed by hand into invoices, attendance sheets, registers and spreadsheets. The portal holds each of those facts once and derives every document and total from them.

Coach payments

Classes and attendance become each coach's monthly invoice and attendance sheet, with a reference number, the treasurer's signature and a record of when it went to OSL and when it was paid.

Membership fees

A term's join-and-pay form, a PayNow QR, members declaring payment with a screenshot, the exco confirming, and the OSL Income Deposit Form at the end.

Records

The roster, classes, coaches and their rates, both transaction registers (OSL and unofficial), and an append-only audit log.

Access

Sign-in by emailed code, Google or Microsoft; invitations, access requests, exco roles and offices; a welcome flow for people not yet on the roster.

Who sees what

Everyone who signs in is in one of three standings, worked out on every request (src/core/auth/standing.ts):

StandingWhoWhat they see
FullExco, or a member whose account is linked to their roster rowExco: everything. Member: home, their own fee and records, their profile.
WelcomeSigned in, not on the roster, hasn't said who they areThe welcome screens, then the join-and-pay form
JoiningHas said who they are and waits for the exco to match themThe join-and-pay form only

Somebody coming back who types a student ID that is on the roster, and not yet anybody's, is linked to that row and moves to Full. The club chose this over waiting for the exco. Because student IDs run in sequence, the lookup that fills in their name and birthday answers only accounts not yet on the roster, eight times an hour and twenty a day, and each answer is audited. Linking never grants a role, never moves a row that is already somebody's, and an exco member can undo it with "unlink login" on the fees board.

02Architecture

One Next.js application on Vercel, talking to one Supabase project. Pages are server components; every write is a server action. The browser never talks to the database directly, and never posts a money figure.

A request, start to finish

  1. Proxy

    src/proxy.ts refreshes the Supabase session cookie, sends anyone signed out to /sign-in (remembering where they were going), and sets a per-request nonce for the Content Security Policy. A button pressed on a signed-out tab gets a redirect the router understands, not an HTML page.

  2. Layout

    [orgSlug]/layout.tsx verifies the session, loads the organisation and membership (core/auth/dal.ts), works out the standing, and builds the navigation for it.

  3. Page

    The page checks its own permission again (a layout is kept across client navigation, so a check there alone is not enough), then reads through dbAs(userId, tx => …).

  4. Action

    A form posts a server action. It calls requirePermission(orgId, key), validates the form with zod, writes inside a transaction, writes an audit row, and revalidates the paths that show the data.

04Signing in and access

There are no passwords. A password proves nothing about the address it was set against, so the portal offers three ways in, all of which prove the person holds the address:

  • A code by email. Codes, not links: Outlook's Safe Links opens every link in an email before the person does, which spends a one-time link.
  • Google, when Google says the address is verified.
  • Microsoft, when the token carries xms_edov or comes from a tenant listed in MICROSOFT_TENANT_IDS.

What happens on sign-in

  1. Proof

    The route that just signed the person in decides whether the address is proven (core/auth/proof.ts). Supabase's own email_confirmed_at is not trusted, because a password sign-up stamps it for any address.

  2. Secure

    The first time an address is proven, any password and every other session on the account are replaced, in case somebody signed up as that address earlier (core/auth/secure.ts).

  3. Provision

    provisionUser mirrors the account into users. A proven address joins the club if it has a pending invitation, or is on an allowed domain (ALLOWED_EMAIL_DOMAINS, default SUTD student, SUTD alumni and Gmail).

  4. Link

    The account is linked to its roster row automatically when the row's email is this address, or the address is a student address whose local part is the row's student ID. Anything ambiguous links nobody. Somebody else coming back links themselves on the welcome screen (below).

  5. Bootstrap

    On an empty database, an address in BOOTSTRAP_EXCO_EMAILS becomes the first exco. This stops working as soon as any active exco exists.

  6. Route

    The person lands on the dashboard, the welcome screens, or "you're not on the list yet", depending on their membership and standing.

The welcome flow

Somebody signed in but not on the roster sees the welcome screens ([orgSlug]/welcome). They say whether they have danced with the club before, then give their name, student ID and birthday, and first-timers give their graduation year. On the returning path the student ID comes first: lookUpStudentId fills in the name and birthday from the roster, and saveWelcome links the account to that row. Any other student ID is stored as a claim for an exco member to confirm. Nothing here grants a role. An exco member can walk through the screens without saving anything at /welcome?preview.

Profile and logins

Everybody has Settings → Profile, on the roster or not: a photo (cropped and shrunk in the browser, kept in the private bucket, served only to its owner by /api/photo), their name and birthday, and their student ID until the roster has them. A member's name and birthday are written to their roster row too and marked as theirs, so an attendance import does not type over the name.

One person has one account. "Connect Google" adds Google as a second way into the same account (Supabase manual linking: switch on "Allow manual linking" under Authentication → Sign In / Providers in the Supabase dashboard). If that Google account already has a login of its own, its owner signs in with it and uses "Remove this login", offered only on a login with no roster row, role, payment or form response; the row is kept for the audit log, switched off, and its address released.

Roles, offices and permissions

There are two roles. Exco can do everything; member can declare their own fee and ask for access. An exco member also holds an office (president, treasurer and so on), which grants nothing: it decides who is told about what, and whose title prints under a signature. Keeping offices out of access means a mid-year reshuffle is a dropdown, not a security decision.

People get in by invitation (an exco member names an address) or by request (somebody signs in and asks). A request can never be approved by the person who made it, and requests to become exco are a separate queue that says what it grants.

The demo sign-in

The hosted demo replaces the sign-in page with four made-up people: the treasurer, the president, a member on the roster, and a newcomer who gets a fresh account every time. It runs only when DEMO_MODE=true and the database holds exactly one club with the slug demo, so a stray setting on the real deployment does nothing (core/auth/demo.ts, tested in demo.int.test.ts).

05Data model

Every table carries org_id, and every row-level policy is written against it. Money is integer cents. 06Security model

The portal assumes the publishable key is public and that any signed-in person may try to call Supabase directly. Each control below holds on its own.

Row-level security, enforced by the connection

dbAs(userId, fn) opens a transaction as app_user, installs the user's claims, and refuses to run if the connection is not actually app_user, because running as the table owner would silently bypass RLS. Hosted Supabase doesn't allow SET ROLE into custom roles, so production connects as app_user directly through APP_USER_DATABASE_URL. It allows at most ten scoped transactions at once, to stay under the pooler's connection limit.

dbAdmin bypasses RLS and is used only inside src/core: provisioning, throttling, audit, roster linking.

Controls in the database itself

  • Separation of duties on fees. A trigger allows a payment to become CONFIRMED (or REJECTED) only by an active exco member of the same club who is not the person who declared it. A payment cannot be inserted already confirmed.
  • Closed terms are frozen. Once a collection closes, its payments, fee decisions and close-out charges can't be changed, and a void invoice can't be brought back to life.
  • Forms follow their state. Guards on the membership form, its questions, recipients and responses stop edits that the form's state doesn't allow.
  • Grants are split. app_user holds table grants that authenticated (the role PostgREST runs as) does not. CI checks the split on every push.

Everything else

  • Files. Screenshots, signatures and QR codes sit in the private private-documents bucket, with no storage policies for signed-in users. The server checks who is asking, derives the object key itself, and streams the bytes. It never hands out a reusable link.
  • Raised months. The app refuses to create, edit or delete a class, or change attendance, in a month that has a live invoice. The attendance import skips those months and lists them.
  • Amounts. The browser never posts a dollar figure. Fees come from the term's rates or an exco decision; claims come from classes × the effective rate.
  • Sensitive fields. Coach bank account numbers are encrypted by the app before they reach the database (core/crypto/fields.ts, keys in FIELD_ENCRYPTION_KEYS).
  • Throttling. Code requests and code checks are limited per address and per IP (core/auth/throttle.ts).
  • Headers. A nonce-based Content Security Policy on every page; no inline script runs without the nonce.
  • Audit and alerts. Every mutation writes an audit row. Access changes, bank or signature changes, financial voids and server errors can also alert by Telegram and email.

07Membership fees

The member side lives at /:org/fees; the exco side at /:org/fees/collection. The one control the whole flow rests on is that the person who says they paid is never the person who confirms it.

  1. Exco

    Create the term with its dates and rates (full, half, exchange). Tell OSL at least two weeks before collection opens so they can set up the DBS Max wallet.

  2. Exco

    Set up the membership form (intro, audience, any extra questions), upload this collection's PayNow QR, and open the form.

  3. Member

    Fill in the form, scan the QR, pay, then declare: the bank's reference plus a screenshot. The amount is worked out by the server. The payment is now DECLARED.

  4. Portal

    If the person is not matched to a roster row yet, the payment is held on their response. When an exco member matches the response to a row, it moves to a FeePayment.

  5. Exco

    Check each declaration against the bank statement and confirm or reject it with a reason. Cash or late payments can be recorded directly, and marked unofficial with a reason if they never went through OSL.

  6. Exco

    Close the collection. The portal writes down who was charged what, allocates an INC reference, and fills in OSL's own Income Deposit Form workbook, due one working day after the final collection.

  7. Exco

    Mark the form as sent to OSL. Closing, sending and the money landing are three separate facts, and each is recorded separately.

How a fee is worked out

Without an exco decision, the fee is read off the roster label, which is free text ("Confirmed", "1/2 term", "Confirmed; Exchange", "Graduated"). The reader is generous and says how sure it is; uncertain labels are flagged on the collection screen before anybody is charged. An exco decision (FeeArrangement) overrides the label: half, waived, or a custom figure. See modules/fees/tiers.ts.

08Coach payments

Coaches issue the invoice to SUTD; the club countersigns it and forwards it to OSL with the attendance sheet, by the 15th of the following month. The run lives at /:org/monthly-run, and the home screen shows it as a job card with the next step.

  1. Classes

    Record each class and mark the whole roster, at /:org/sessions, or import the trimester sheet. A date with no coach is self-practice: it appears on the attendance sheet and on no invoice.

  2. Raise

    One action per coach per month: check the coach can be invoiced (payee name, bank account), allocate the next IV number in the same transaction, price every class at the rate in effect on its date, and freeze a snapshot of everything the documents say.

  3. Read

    Read both documents on screen. The preview is tested to say exactly what the .docx says.

  4. Sign

    One button verifies the attendance sheet and countersigns the invoice. The signer's name and office are captured at that moment, and their stored signature is stamped if they have one. A coach's signature is never stamped by the club.

  5. Send

    Download the pack (or print it to one PDF), and the portal opens an email to OSL with the reference, amount and fund filled in. Mark it sent.

  6. Paid

    Mark it paid when the money reaches the coach. A mistake after raising is fixed by voiding the month, which keeps the old number void, and raising it again with a new one.

09Attendance import

The club keeps attendance in one Google Sheet per trimester. The treasurer exports it as CSV and uploads it at Settings → Attendance; the portal never holds standing access to the club's Drive.

  1. Parse

    Read the roster block and the date grid. Dates have no year, so they are resolved inside the chosen term; a date outside it is an error, not a guess. The class-length legend is read from the sheet.

  2. Match

    Match rows to roster members by student ID, then email, then an exact unique name. Everything unmatched is shown before anything is written.

  3. Map dates

    The sheet's instructor rows say who taught each date. The treasurer confirms each one as a coach's class, self-practice, or left out.

  4. Apply

    Write classes and marks. Months that already have a raised invoice are left alone and listed.

Every import keeps the sheet it read, so scripts/db/reapply-import.ts can re-run a stored parse under new rules without the original file.

10Money and references

Reference numbers

Four series, each numbered independently and reset to 0001 every financial year (April to March). The hyphens are part of the format.

SeriesDocumentExample
IVCoach invoiceIV26-097-0010
INCIncome Deposit FormINC26-097-0001
SCFStudent expense claimSCF26-097-0001
POPurchase orderPO26-097-0001

A number is allocated by one atomic UPDATE … RETURNING inside the transaction that writes the document, so two people can never get the same one. Issued numbers are never deleted: voiding marks the number void with a reason, so every gap in a series can be explained. FY2026's IV counter opens at 15, because 0001 to 0012 were issued before the portal and 0013 and 0014 went to the August invoices. The counter first opened at 10, and those two invoices had to be renumbered from 0010 and 0011 when Finance pointed out that June and July had used 0009 to 0012.

GST

GST is derived, never typed. From a gross amount: net = gross × 10000 / (10000 + rate), and GST is the remainder, so net + GST always equals gross to the cent. The rate is a setting in basis points (900 for 9%).

Funds

Each fund is three facts per financial year: what it opened at, what came in, and what went out. Coach invoices draw on the OSL Seed fund (does not roll over) or the Income fund. The Unofficial fund is money the exco hold outside OSL; it shows on the dashboard but no invoice can draw on it.

14Operations

Deploying

  1. Once

    Create app_user with scripts/db/create-app-user.sql and give it a login with scripts/db/set-app-user-password.sh. Create the private bucket with scripts/create-storage-bucket.ts.

  2. Each release

    Apply migrations first with npm run db:deploy. The build never runs them.

  3. Each release

    Merge to main. Vercel builds and deploys production.

  4. After

    Run npm run db:verify-rls and sign in once to check.

Backups and recovery

The targets are a 24-hour recovery point and a four-hour recovery time. Supabase Pro keeps seven days of daily database backups; the Free plan keeps none and pauses idle projects, so it is fit for the demo, not for live money. A database backup does not include Storage objects, so the private bucket needs its own encrypted copy. Rehearse a restore into a separate project before launch and every quarter.

Until the live project is on Pro, the treasurer's laptop keeps the copies. scripts/db/backup.sh saves the public schema with pg_dump into ~/Backups/FunktionPortal, keeping thirty, and scripts/db/install-backup-agent.sh makes it run at login and every evening at 21:00. A copy restores with pg_restore --no-owner --no-privileges into a database that already has the authenticated, anon, app_user and service_role roles. It does not include the Supabase auth schema or the bucket's files.

Something wrong in one record

Void it in the portal with a reason, and restore it the same way. Never edit history.

A bulk mistake

Restore the nearest backup beside production, find exactly what changed, and copy back only that, after a second person checks the plan.

Everything is gone

Stop writes, restore through Supabase, rotate secrets, run the checks below, then reopen.

After any restore, check that: sign-in works and members and exco are still separated; the audit log is intact on both sides of the incident; reference numbers have no duplicates; register totals match the spreadsheet and bank; private files open only for the right people; alerts deliver; and db:verify-rls, the integration tests and the browser smoke test pass. There is deliberately no "restore the portal" button in the app: one stolen exco session could use it to erase everyone else's work.

Logs and alerts

The audit log is the business record. Runtime logs are structured JSON and never contain screenshots, bank details, student IDs, tokens or full requests. Set SECURITY_ALERT_EMAILS to the treasurer, the president and the technical owner. Write down who may ask for a restore, who approves it, and who holds the database, Vercel, email and encryption keys.
